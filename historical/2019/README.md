# HESI 2019 Housing-and-Food Snapshot — Preliminary Historical Comparison

## Results

The 2019 analysis processed:

- 3,239,553 Census person records.
- 1,276,716 occupied household records.
- 1,253,821 eligible household records, representing approximately 120.69 million households.
- 100% household person-count matching.

Households paying no cash rent were excluded to maintain the pilot's analytical definition.

## Preliminary 2019–2024 Comparison

Percentage of eligible households whose gross income falls below reported housing costs plus modeled USDA food costs:

| Food Plan | Household Group | 2019 | 2024 | Change |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Low-Cost | National | 9.243% | 10.515% | +1.272 pp |
| Low-Cost | Renters | 16.645% | 17.961% | +1.316 pp |
| Low-Cost | Homeowners | 5.307% | 6.751% | +1.444 pp |
| Thrifty | National | 8.350% | 10.301% | +1.951 pp |
| Thrifty | Renters | 15.001% | 17.593% | +2.592 pp |
| Thrifty | Homeowners | 4.813% | 6.613% | +1.800 pp |

## Methodology

The analysis uses 2019 ACS 1-Year PUMS household and person records, October 2019 USDA food-cost estimates, and Census household survey weights with 80 replicate weights.

## Important Limitations

- The 2019 analysis uses October food prices, while 2024 uses December prices.
- USDA revised the Thrifty Food Plan methodology in 2021, limiting direct historical comparability.
- Food expenses are modeled rather than observed.
- Transportation, healthcare, childcare, taxes, other essentials, savings and assets are excluded.
- These findings do not represent the completed five-level HESI.
- Further methodological harmonization is required before publishing definitive historical conclusions.

## Research Status

**Preliminary historical analysis.**

The initial results are consistent with increased housing-and-food affordability pressure between 2019 and 2024, but they do not yet establish a fully harmonized economic-security trend.
